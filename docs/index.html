Method and caveats
  • One row per Beige Book release. The outcome is the change in the announced target between the release date and 21 days later, which captures the FOMC meeting about two weeks after publication. The upper bound of the range is used after December 2008.
  • Sentiment is scored on each of the twelve district reports and averaged. Scores are z-scored against a trailing window of 40 releases, so no future information leaks in. Directional tone counts up and down words ("rose", "declined") separately in activity sentences and in price and wage sentences. Those word lists are hand-written, not a published lexicon.
  • Models are ridge regressions refit at every release on earlier data only. Predictions are floored so that no cut is predicted when the target is already at the bound. The gated models also switch sentiment off while the target is at the bound.
  • The FOMC began announcing its target in February 1994. Earlier values come from the Fed's reconstruction. Before September 1982 there is no target, so those years are used only to warm up the rolling averages.
  • There are about 100 non-hold decisions in the test period. The directional feature sets and the zero-lower-bound treatment were chosen after seeing earlier results, so the intervals understate the real uncertainty. Data after May 2020 would be a clean test.
  • Sources: Beige Book archive (Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis); FRED series FEDFUNDS, DFEDTAR, DFEDTARU and DFEDTARL.
